FilmRoll, a Digital Disposable Camera for Weddings

I love disposable cameras at weddings, but they get lost and the photos wait weeks. I designed and built FilmRoll, a shared camera roll guests join in seconds.

Try FilmRoll Live

Problem

Disposable cameras are expensive to buy for every table, easy to lose, and the photos stay locked until someone develops the film.

Solution

One shared digital roll guests join with a code or QR, with film-stock looks so every photo still feels like a disposable.

Goal

Get more guests shooting, and everyone seeing the photos the next morning.

Role Product design, UX/UI, built solo · Tools Figma, Base44 · Platform Mobile web

Research & Principles

The starting point was a real annoyance: disposable cameras that disappear and photos nobody sees for weeks. Three rules came out of it:

  • Keep the limits: a few film looks, not an editing suite
  • Remove the friction, not the ritual: no download, no sign-up
  • Shared by default: one roll for the whole event

Process & Decisions

  • A camera, not a filter picker, with named film stocks
  • Retro date-stamps on every shot
  • Join in one tap with a code, a QR or a link
  • One shared roll with a "Find me in photos" tool

How I thought it through

Key screens

Every screen feels like a physical object first and software second.

Tested at a real wedding

I designed and built FilmRoll myself in Base44, then handed it to the guests at May & Yoav's wedding, who filled one shared roll over the night.

What I took away

Constraints give a product character. The limits are what make it feel special.

No-code is still design work. Every flow still had to be designed and argued for.

Nothing tests like a real event. Real guests showed things no mockup could.
