Designing a Self-Order Kiosk for Chido

Chido is a Mexican street food restaurant. I designed its full self-order flow for a portrait touchscreen, in Hebrew, from the welcome screen to the order number.

Problem

Ordering at a busy counter is slow, and a long menu with sauces and extras is easy to get wrong.

Solution

A short, photo-first flow: one choice per screen, food photos up front, and extras picked by tapping pictures.

Goal

Faster orders with fewer mistakes, and a bigger order on average.

Role UX/UI design · Tools Figma · Platform Kiosk, 1080 × 1920, Hebrew

User Research

Who stands in front of the kiosk, and what slows them down

The whole order in 25 seconds

From walking up to the kiosk to the order number, in one short flow.

Information Architecture

Before any screen, I mapped the kiosk as a sitemap: welcome, dine in or take away, then six menu categories and one checkout.

  • Simple items go straight to the dish
  • Tacos and burritos split into ready made or build your own
  • One checkout for every path

Process & Decisions

  • One decision per screen, with big buttons
  • Food photos lead, text comes second
  • Sauces and extras picked from photo tiles, not lists
  • The total and "Pay" always visible at the bottom

How I thought it through

From welcome to dish

Cream paper, green buttons with a yellow outline, and the corn mascot where a guest might hesitate.

Brand & mascot

A retro, playful identity: a chunky yellow wordmark, a script tagline and a corn mascot in sunglasses.

What I took away

A kiosk is a first impression. The brand and the photos set the tone of the meal.

Design for the queue. Big targets and one decision at a time keep it fast.

Wireframes first. Settling the structure in gray let the brand go on top without changes.
